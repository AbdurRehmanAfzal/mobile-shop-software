## 1. What is Mobile Shop?

Mobile Shop is an app that runs a Pakistani mobile shop: buying and selling phones (new and used), udhaar khata, returns, repairs, chargers and covers, daily expenses and the day's hisaab. It works on a phone, a tablet or a laptop, in **English, Urdu or Roman Urdu**.

It has been live since **27 October 2026** at **https://mobileshop.oopsolutions.com**, and there is also an **Android app**.

### The problem it solves

Most mobile shops still run on a diary, a register or a loose Excel sheet. That causes the same problems again and again:

- **Udhaar gets lost.** Someone took a phone on half payment three months ago. Whose name was it in? How much is left? The diary page is torn, or the salesman wrote it somewhere else.
- **Used phones are risky.** A walk-in customer sells you a phone. A week later the police come because it was stolen. You have no copy of his CNIC, no photo, nothing to show you bought it honestly.
- **Nobody knows who did what.** Which salesman sold the A54 for Rs. 2,000 less? Who put that phone in stock?
- **"Galle mein kitna hona chahiye?"** At closing time the cash in the drawer does not match, and nobody knows why.
- **A phone's history is lost.** The same phone comes back to the shop after six months. Did we buy it before? Who sold it to us? Was there a problem?
- **Hard software.** Existing software is in English, looks like an accounting program, and expects the shopkeeper to know accounting words.

Mobile Shop answers these with simple screens, big buttons, plain shop words and automatic totals. The shopkeeper never adds up anything by hand.

## 2. Who is it for?

| Person | What they do in the app |
|---|---|
| **Shop owner (maalik)** | Sees everything: today's sale, profit after expenses, who owes what, stock worth, every report. Adds staff, sets their role, changes shop settings, cancels a wrong bill. |
| **Manager** | Runs the counter like the owner: sells, buys, sees buying prices and profit, cancels bills, records expenses. Cannot add staff or change shop settings. |
| **Salesman** | Sells, adds stock, takes money, takes in repairs. Does **not** see buying prices or profit, and cannot cancel bills or record expenses. |
| **Technician (karigar)** | Works on repair jobs. |
| **Product owner (super admin)** | OOP Solutions. Opens a new shop for each customer and gives the owner their login. |

**There is no self sign-up.** A shop cannot create its own account from the login page. A shopkeeper who wants the app contacts OOP Solutions (the number is on the login page), and the product owner opens the shop for them. This keeps control over who uses the service.

## 3. The main features

Each feature below says what it does, why a shop needs it, and gives a short example.

### 3.1 Home: the shop counter

**What it does.** The home screen answers the owner's daily questions straight away, without opening any report:

- **Search** at the top: type an IMEI, a phone model or a person's name, or scan an IMEI barcode.
- **Big buttons for the main jobs:** Sell, Add stock, and then Udhaar, Stock, Repair, Expense and Return.
- **Hisaab card** for any period: Today, Yesterday, Week, This month, or any From–To dates. It shows the total sale, how much was given on udhaar, cash received, and **"Left in hand"** (money that came in minus money that went out). It also explains *why* money went out (for example "spent on stock", "given back on returns", "expenses").
- **Udhaar khata:** two cards, **"I will get"** (people owe the shop) and **"I have to give"** (the shop owes dealers or others).
- **Stock in shop:** the worth of all phones in stock, shown in lakh when large. Tap it to see stock by brand, then by model, with count and value.
- **Profit** (owner and manager only).
- **Needs attention:** phones whose ID card check is still pending, and repair jobs that are still open.

**Why it matters.** The owner can stand at the counter, or sit at home, and know in five seconds how the shop is doing.

**Example.** At 9 pm Imran opens the app on his phone. Today: sale Rs. 186,000, udhaar given Rs. 25,000, cash received Rs. 161,000. "I will get" shows Rs. 340,000 from 12 people. Stock in shop: Rs. 21.5 lakh.

### 3.2 Add stock (buying phones)

**What it does.** Adding stock takes three steps:

1. **From whom?** A **dealer** (wholesaler or distributor, usually new or box-pack phones) or a **customer's phone** (someone came to sell their own phone).
2. **Which phone, how much?** Brand, model, memory, colour (hundreds of models are already in the list, and you can add your own), IMEI (type it or scan the barcode on the box), condition (box pack, open box, used, patched), PTA status (PTA approved, Non-PTA, JV, with SIM time for Non-PTA/JV), warranty, buying price and selling price. Then **what came with the phone**: box, charger, cable, earphones, AirPods. For each one there are three big buttons: **No · Got it · Later**. "Later" asks when the seller will give it, so the shop remembers what is still owed.
3. **How did you pay?** Cash, Easypaisa/JazzCash/bank account, or udhaar (with "paid now" if part was paid). You can also say *when* it was bought, if you are entering it later.

**ID card check for a customer's phone.** When a walk-in sells a used phone, the app asks you to check that it is not stolen:
- CNIC number, photos of the CNIC front and back, a photo of the seller and a photo of the phone;
- a button that opens the Punjab Police **eGadget** portal, where you check the IMEI and record the result;
- the PTA check (by SMS to 8484);
- a printable **declaration** that the seller signs, saying the phone is his.

If you know the person well, you can tap **"Skip, I know this person"**. The app saves who skipped it, in the phone's history. If a phone is marked **"Stolen? Do not sell"**, only the owner or manager can still sell it, and they must write a reason.

**Old stock.** When a shop starts using the app, phones already in the shop are added as "Old stock" (no dealer, no cash). Old udhaar from the diary is added as an opening balance on each person.

**Example.** Bilal buys a used Galaxy A54 from a walk-in customer, Usman, for Rs. 52,000 cash. He takes photos of Usman's CNIC, Usman's face and the phone, checks the IMEI on eGadget (clear), prints the declaration and gets it signed. The charger came with the phone, the box did not ("No"). He sets the selling price at Rs. 58,000. The A54 is now in stock.

### 3.3 Selling (cash or udhaar)

**What it does.** Selling also takes three steps: **tap the phone → price and how it was paid → done.**

- Payment can be Cash, an account (Easypaisa, JazzCash, bank), **Udhaar**, or **part udhaar** (some now, the rest later). The customer's name is asked only when there is udhaar.
- Warranty chips: None, 7 days, 1, 3 or 6 months, 1 year.
- Accessories: **No · Given · Later**, with a date for "Later", so the shop remembers a promised charger or box.
- Chargers, covers or other items can be added to the same bill.
- At the end you can print an **80 mm receipt** or an **A4 bill**.

**Below-cost rule.** A phone's selling price can never be set below its buying price. At the sale, if someone types a price below the buying price, the app shows a red warning. A salesman is told to raise the price. Only the owner or manager can tap **"I know, sell at a loss"**.

**Example.** Two days later Bilal sells the A54 to Ahmed Raza for Rs. 58,000: Rs. 29,000 now in cash, Rs. 29,000 on udhaar. Ahmed's khata now shows Rs. 29,000 to get, and the shop's profit on this phone is Rs. 6,000.

### 3.4 Returns (wapsi) with the shop's cut

**What it does.** When a customer brings a phone back, you find it by IMEI or name (or from recent sales), tick what came back, and enter how much to give back. The app works out the **shop's cut** automatically. The money can be given back from cash or an account, or kept in the customer's khata. The phone goes back into stock, and the day's hisaab shows "given back on returns" and "cut on returns".

**Why it matters.** Pakistani shops usually keep a cut when a phone comes back. The app records it honestly as shop income, not as a mystery difference in the drawer.

**Example.** A customer bought a phone for Rs. 100,000 and returns it the next day. The shop gives back Rs. 90,000. The app records Rs. 10,000 as the shop's cut, and the phone is back in stock, ready to sell again.

### 3.5 Udhaar khata (people and balances)

**What it does.** One list of everyone the shop deals with: customers, dealers, other shops and technicians. Each person has a page with their balance ("I will get" or "I have to give"), a **khata history** of every sale, purchase and payment, and big **Money received** / **Money paid** buttons. The balance is always worked out from the records; nobody types it by hand. You can filter the list, search by name, phone or CNIC, and download a person's khata.

**Example.** Ahmed Raza pays Rs. 15,000 on Friday and Rs. 14,000 the next week. Each time the shop taps "Money received". After the second payment his balance shows **Clear**.

### 3.6 IMEI history and barcode scan

**What it does.**
- Every phone has a **full story** ("Puri kahani"): bought from whom, ID checked, given on amanat, sold, returned, sent for repair, price changed, with the date, the person, the amount and the staff member who did it.
- **IMEI check:** type or scan an IMEI and the app says "New IMEI, not seen before" or "This IMEI has been in your shop before, bought 2 times". It also warns if an IMEI looks wrong (bad check digit).
- **Barcode scan:** next to every IMEI box there is a **Scan** button. Point the phone's camera at the box label, and the app reads the IMEI and ignores the other barcodes on the box. If the label has IMEI 1 and IMEI 2, it lets you pick.

**Why it matters.** If the police or a customer ever ask about a phone, the shop can show exactly where it came from and where it went. The history cannot be erased.

**Example.** A man brings a Redmi Note 13 to sell. Bilal scans it: "This IMEI has been in your shop before". The history shows the shop sold it to the same man eight months ago, with a 6-month warranty.

### 3.7 Repairs (marammat)

**What it does.** A repair job follows four stages: **Taken in → Being fixed → Ready → Given back.**
- Take in: customer, phone, problem (quick-pick chips plus your own words), likely cost, advance, ready-by date. Print a **job slip**.
- "It is fixed": add parts (from the shop's parts stock) and the work charge.
- "Give back to customer": shows what is left to take; anything not paid goes on the customer's khata.
- Also: "waiting for parts", "could not fix", and the shop's own phones sent to an outside technician (the technician's charge is added to that phone's cost).

**Example.** Sana leaves her iPhone 11 for a screen change. Likely cost Rs. 8,500, advance Rs. 2,000, ready in 3 days. The screen is fitted and the job is marked ready. She pays Rs. 5,000 when she collects it, and Rs. 1,500 stays on her khata.

### 3.8 Accessories (chargers, covers and more)

**What it does.** Stock of chargers, cables, covers, glass protectors, earphones, batteries, memory cards and repair parts, with selling price, average cost and a "low stock" level. Accessories can be sold on their own or on the same bill as a phone. There is also a **pending accessories** list: what dealers still owe the shop and what the shop still owes customers, with a "Delivered" button.

**Example.** The shop sets "low stock" for 20W chargers at 5. When only 4 are left, they show as low stock, so the owner reorders before running out.

### 3.9 Expenses and close the day

**What it does.** Record expenses by category (rent, electricity, salaries, advances, tea and others; you can add your own). At closing, **Close the day** shows the opening cash, cash in, cash out and **how much should be in the drawer**. The shopkeeper counts the cash and types it, and the app shows the difference. Expenses are recorded by the owner or manager.

**Example.** Rent Rs. 45,000, tea Rs. 600, electricity bill Rs. 12,400. At night the app says Rs. 83,200 should be in the drawer. The manager counts Rs. 83,000, so the difference of Rs. 200 is noted.

### 3.10 Exchange (old for new)

**What it does.** A customer gives an old phone and takes a new one in a single step. The app works out: new phone price − old phone value + any previous balance − amount paid = new balance. The old phone goes into stock as a purchase from that customer (with its own ID check).

**Example.** Kashif gives his Vivo Y21 (valued at Rs. 18,000) and takes a Galaxy A15 for Rs. 42,000. He pays Rs. 20,000 cash, so Rs. 4,000 goes on his khata.

### 3.11 Loans to other shops (amanat)

**What it does.** A neighbouring shop often takes a phone "to show a customer". The app marks the phone **"On loan"** to that shop (not in stock, not sold), with an expected return date, and flags it as overdue if late. Later you tap either **"Phone came back"** or **"They kept it"**. "They kept it" turns it into a sale to that shop at the agreed price, which goes on their khata.

**Example.** Al-Madina Mobiles takes a Tecno Spark 20 on amanat. Two days later they keep it at Rs. 31,000 and pay Rs. 15,000. Their khata shows Rs. 16,000 to get.

### 3.12 Reports

**What it does.** For owner and manager, with ranges like Today, Week, This month or any From–To dates:
- **Profit:** sales, cost of phones sold, repair income, other income (such as cuts on returns), expenses, and **real profit after expenses**. Tax collected is shown too.
- **Best sellers:** which phone models and items sold most, and the profit on each.
- **Estimate:** a guess for the next 7 days, based on the same weekdays over the last 4 weeks, plus which phones are about to run out ("days left") with advice: buy more / OK / slow. It is clearly labelled as a guess, not a promise.
- **Old stock:** how long phones have been sitting in the shop.
- **Sales tax:** taxable sales and tax collected, if the shop charges sales tax (rate, prices with or without tax, NTN/STRN on the bill are set in Settings).

**Downloads.** Every report and list (day hisaab, stock, stock worth, khata, a person's khata, sales, purchases, expenses) has a **Download** button with three choices: **PDF**, **Excel** and **CSV** (a simple table file that any spreadsheet opens).

### 3.13 Staff, roles and "who did it"

**What it does.** The owner adds staff with a role (owner, manager, salesman, technician), can reset their password, and can switch them off when they leave. Each staff member can set a **quick PIN** (4 to 6 digits) for fast login on the shop's shared phone. Every sale and stock entry shows **who sold it** and **who added it**.

**Example.** The owner sees that the Galaxy S23 was sold by Hamza at 4:15 pm, and the iPhone 13 was added to stock by Zubair yesterday.

### 3.14 Messages to the owner and customers

**What it does.** In settings, the shop chooses who gets a message and when: the owner on every sale or stock entry, the customer on a sale or payment, and the seller on a purchase. A "repair ready" message is also prepared. Today the app prepares each message and the shopkeeper sends it on WhatsApp with one tap. Fully automatic sending is planned (see section 6).

### 3.15 Three languages, light and dark

- **English, Urdu and Roman Urdu.** Only one language shows at a time. Urdu is written in proper Nastaliq, right to left. Numbers are always in normal digits (1, 2, 3).
- Printed receipts and the declaration follow the shop's chosen language (English or Urdu).
- **Light and Dark** mode with one tap from the home screen.
- Every button has an icon *and* a word, so nobody has to guess.

### 3.16 Super admin (for OOP Solutions)

Only the product owner sees this page. It lists every customer shop and has **"Open a new shop"**: shop name, owner's name, the owner's login mobile and password, and shop language. At the end it shows the login details to give the owner, with a **Send on WhatsApp** button.

### 3.17 Phone app, website and laptop

- **Website:** open https://mobileshop.oopsolutions.com in any browser on a phone, tablet or laptop. On Android Chrome you can also "Add to Home screen" so it opens like an app.
- **Android app (APK):** an APK is an Android app file you install directly, without the Play Store. It has been installed and tested on a real phone against the live system. *Current limit:* printing and downloading do not work inside the Android app yet; use the website for those.
- **Laptop:** on a big screen the jobs appear as a side menu, with keyboard shortcuts (F-keys) for fast counter work.

## 4. A typical day with Mobile Shop

*Hamza works at Bilal Mobiles in Saddar. Bilal, the owner, is on his way from home.*

- **10:00. Opening.** Hamza logs in with his PIN on the counter phone. The home screen shows "Needs attention: 1 phone, ID card check pending" from yesterday evening. He opens it, adds the seller's CNIC photos, and the check is done.
- **11:30. A dealer delivery.** Five box-pack Infinix Hot 40 phones come from the dealer at Rs. 33,500 each. Hamza scans each IMEI from the box, picks "From a dealer", and enters Rs. 100,000 paid in cash and the rest on udhaar. The dealer's khata now shows **Rs. 67,500 to give**.
- **12:15. A walk-in sells his phone.** Usman sells a used Galaxy A54 for Rs. 52,000. Hamza takes CNIC and photos, checks eGadget (clear), prints the declaration and gets it signed.
- **14:00. Sale on half udhaar.** Ahmed Raza, a regular, takes an Infinix Hot 40 for Rs. 38,000: Rs. 19,000 cash, Rs. 19,000 udhaar. Hamza prints the receipt.
- **15:30. A repair.** Sana leaves her phone for a screen change: likely cost Rs. 8,500, advance Rs. 2,000. A job slip is printed.
- **16:00. A return.** A customer returns yesterday's Rs. 40,000 phone. The shop gives back Rs. 37,000. The Rs. 3,000 cut is recorded and the phone is back in stock.
- **17:00. Bilal arrives** and opens the app on his own phone. He sees today's sale, who sold what, and that Hamza tried to sell a phone below cost. The app blocked it, so Bilal approved it himself at Rs. 500 below cost to clear old stock.
- **20:30. Expenses.** Tea Rs. 400, Hamza's advance Rs. 3,000.
- **21:30. Close the day.** The app says what should be in the drawer. Bilal counts it, enters the amount, and the day is closed. At home he checks **Reports → Profit** for the month and **Estimate**, which says Infinix Hot 40 will run out in about 4 days, so he calls the dealer.

## 5. Safety and trust

- **Each shop's data is separate.** Every shop sees only its own phones, people and money. One shop can never see another shop's data.
- **Nightly backups.** The system takes a full backup every night and keeps the last 14 days. (Keeping an extra copy outside the server is planned.)
- **The khata is never edited.** Money records are never changed or deleted. If a mistake is made, a **new correcting entry** is added and the original stays. Balances are always worked out from these records, so they can always be explained.
- **A phone's history can't be erased.** Every event (bought, checked, sold, returned, loaned, repaired) is saved permanently. Deleting a person or item that has history only hides it; the records stay safe.
- **Owner-only actions.** Cancelling a bill, recording expenses, seeing buying prices and profit, selling below cost, and selling a phone marked "Stolen?" are limited to the owner and manager. Adding staff and changing shop settings are for the owner only. Each of these needs a reason where it matters, and the name of the person who did it is saved.
- **Proof for used phones.** CNIC photos, the seller's photo, the eGadget result and the signed declaration stay with the phone forever.
- **Secure connection.** The website uses a secure (https) connection, the same kind banks use, so data travelling between the phone and the server is protected.
